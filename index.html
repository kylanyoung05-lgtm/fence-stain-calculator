Fence stain calculation formula

For a simple solid fence, start with the fence's face area: length × height × number of sides. Multiply that by the number of coats, add your allowance for waste and absorption, then divide by the product's coverage rate.

Gallons needed = (length × height × sides × coats × (1 + waste)) ÷ coverage

Why wood condition matters

New, rough-sawn or weathered wood can absorb considerably more stain than smooth or previously sealed wood. That's why this calculator lets you choose a practical coverage assumption instead of pretending every gallon covers the same amount.

Example

A 100-foot fence that is 6 feet tall and stained on both sides has 1,200 square feet of face area. With two coats, the project covers 2,400 square feet before waste. At 175 square feet per gallon and a 10% allowance, the estimate is about 16 gallons.

Before you buy

  • Check the coverage rate printed on your exact stain.
  • Measure each fence run if the height changes.
  • Decide whether both faces, posts and rails will be coated.
  • For a large job, buying enough from the same product batch can help keep color consistent.

Frequently asked questions

How much stain do I need for a 100-foot fence?

It depends on height, sides, coats and product coverage. A 100-foot, 6-foot fence has 600 square feet on one face or 1,200 square feet on both faces before coats.

How much stain does a 5-gallon bucket cover?

Multiply 5 gallons by the coverage rate on your product label. For example, at 175 square feet per gallon, a 5-gallon bucket represents about 875 square feet for one coat under that assumption.

Should I stain one side or both sides?

If both faces are exposed and you want both protected and finished, choose both sides. That roughly doubles the face area.

How much extra stain should I buy?

A 10% planning allowance is a reasonable starting point, but rough wood, overspray, gates, posts, rails and irregular terrain can increase actual use.
