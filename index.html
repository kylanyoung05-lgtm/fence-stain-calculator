How to calculate fence stain or paint

Start with the fence face area: length × height × number of sides. Multiply that area by the number of coats. Add an allowance for posts, rails, absorption, overspray, and other project waste, then divide by the product's coverage rate.

Gallons needed = coated area × allowances ÷ coverage per gallon

Example: 100-foot fence

A 100-foot fence that is 6 feet high has 600 square feet on one face and 1,200 square feet on both faces. At two coats, the base coating area is 2,400 square feet before any additional allowance.

Why coverage matters

Coverage is one of the biggest variables in a fence coating estimate. Rough, bare, or weathered wood can use more material than a smooth surface. Spraying can also create more loss than careful brushing or rolling. For the best estimate, use the coverage rate supplied for the exact product you plan to buy.

Frequently asked questions

How much stain do I need for a 100-foot fence?

It depends on fence height, how many sides you are coating, the number of coats, and the product's coverage. A 100-foot by 6-foot fence is 600 square feet per side.

Should I stain both sides of my fence?

If both faces will be coated, select “Both sides.” If you are only finishing one face, select “1 side.”

How much extra stain should I buy?

A 10% planning allowance is a reasonable starting point, but rough wood, overspray, gates, posts, rails, and touch-ups can change actual usage.

Is this calculator exact?

No. It is a planning estimate. Always check the manufacturer's coverage and application instructions for the specific product you use.
